Understanding the Stages of Dementia

1

Early-Stage Dementia (Mild)

  • Forgetfulness
  • Losing items
  • Difficulty with tasks
2

Middle-Stage Dementia (Moderate)

  • Worsening short-term memory
  • Incontinence
  • Aphasia
  • Personality changes
  • Wandering
3

Severe Functional Decline

  • Significant personality changes
  • Loss of awareness of surroundings
  • Communication difficulties
  • Increased susceptibility to infections, particularly pneumonia

Early-Stage Dementia (Mild)

Early-stage dementia symptoms are mild and not easily noticeable. At this stage, your loved one is still relatively independent, with only minor impairments in memory, organizational skills, language, or behavior.

Care Services Include:

  • Cognitive stimulation therapy
  • Memory training
  • Emotional support
  • Daily living assistance

Middle-Stage Dementia (Moderate)

Middle-stage dementia symptoms can last for years. At this stage, your loved one will need a higher level of care, as brain degeneration may make it difficult for the patient to express their thoughts, and activities will require more assistance.

Care Services Include:

  • Behavior management
  • Safety monitoring
  • Personal hygiene assistance
  • Medication management

Late-Stage Dementia (Severe)

At this stage, your loved one may have lost the ability to respond to their surroundings and engage in conversation, and may eventually lose the ability to care for themselves and move around. As dementia worsens, the patient will need comprehensive care.

Care Services Include:

  • 24-hour comprehensive care
  • Feeding assistance
  • Physical care
  • Comfort care
